Title: A global workspace in language models

URL Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace

Markdown Content:
[Video 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rKV5JcALQoQ)

As you read this sentence, circuits in your brain are adjusting your posture, controlling your breathing, and transforming lines and curves on the screen into recognizable words. Most of this processing is invisible to you. But some of what takes place in your brain you _do_ have access to—an image that pops into your head, or a deliberate plan you make about where to go shopping. Neuroscientists and philosophers sometimes refer to the latter type of brain activity as "consciously accessible," to distinguish it from all the other processing that goes on unconsciously. This activity has special properties: we can describe it, control it, and use it for deliberate reasoning, in contrast to all the automatic processing that goes on without our awareness.
当你阅读这句话时，大脑中的神经回路正在调整你的姿势、控制你的呼吸，并将屏幕上的线条和曲线转化为可识别的文字。这些处理过程大多对你不可见。但有一部分大脑活动是你确实能感知到的——比如脑海中浮现的一幅画面，或你刻意规划的购物路线。神经科学家和哲学家有时将后一类大脑活动称为"可意识访问的（consciously accessible）"，以区别于所有在无意识中进行的其他处理。这类活动具有特殊属性：我们可以描述它、控制它，并将其用于有意识的推理，而非像自动处理那样在我们不知情的情况下运作。

In a new paper, we present evidence that a similar distinction has emerged in modern language models like Claude. We find that Claude has developed a small collection of internal neural patterns that, compared to all its other internal processing, play a special role.
在一篇新论文中，我们提出证据表明，现代语言模型（如 Claude）中也出现了类似的区分。我们发现，Claude 形成了一小组内部神经模式，与其所有其他内部处理相比，这些模式发挥着特殊作用。

We call the collection of these patterns the _J-space_—named after the technique we used to find them, involving a mathematical concept called the Jacobian. Each J-space pattern is linked to a particular word. But when one of these patterns lights up, it doesn't mean the model is _saying_ that word—just that the word is on its mind. If you've heard of language models having a "scratchpad" or "chain of thought"—text they write to themselves while reasoning—the J-space is something different. It operates silently, in the model's internal neural activations, allowing the model to think about a concept without writing it down. Notably, the J-space wasn't designed or programmed by us, but instead _emerged on its own_ during Claude's training process.
我们将这组模式的集合称为 _J-空间（J-space）_——命名来源于我们用来发现它的技术，该技术涉及一个名为雅可比矩阵（Jacobian）的数学概念。每个 J-空间模式都与某个特定词语相关联。但当其中某个模式被激活时，并不意味着模型正在"说出"那个词，而只是说那个词正处于模型的"心智"之中。如果你听说过语言模型有"草稿本"或"思维链"——即它们在推理时写给自己的文字——那么 J-空间与之不同：它静默地运作于模型的内部神经激活中，让模型无需写下便能思考某个概念。值得注意的是，J-空间并非由我们设计或编程而来，而是在 Claude 的训练过程中自发涌现的。

We find that the J-space has a number of unique properties, compared to the rest of Claude's processing:
与 Claude 其他处理过程相比，我们发现 J-空间具有若干独特属性：

*   Claude can report on these representations. If you ask Claude what it's thinking about, it will tell you what's in the J-space. Non-J-space representations are less reportable.｜Claude 能够报告这些表征。如果你问 Claude 正在想什么，它会告诉你 J-空间中的内容；而非 J-空间的表征则难以被报告。
*   It can also modulate them on request. If you ask Claude to think about something, or solve a problem silently in its head, it will light up the appropriate patterns in its J-space. By contrast, it has trouble modulating patterns not in the J-space.｜它还能按需调节这些表征。如果你要求 Claude 思考某件事，或在脑海中默默解决问题，它会在 J-空间中激活相应模式；相比之下，它很难调节不在 J-空间中的模式。
*   Claude uses its J-space for internal reasoning. If you ask Claude to solve a problem that requires multiple steps, the intermediate steps will light up in its J-space, even when it doesn't say them out loud. These J-space patterns causally mediate its performance in such tasks, despite being smaller in magnitude than other representations.｜Claude 利用 J-空间进行内部推理。如果你要求 Claude 解决一个多步骤问题，中间步骤会在 J-空间中被激活，即便 Claude 并未将其说出。这些 J-空间模式对此类任务的表现具有因果中介作用，尽管其量级小于其他表征。
*   Representations in the J-space can be used flexibly for many tasks—for example, once "France" has lit up in Claude's J-space, the model can recall its capital, or its national currency, or the continent it belongs to.｜J-空间中的表征可以灵活地用于多种任务——例如，一旦"法国"在 Claude 的 J-空间中被激活，模型便可以回忆起其首都、国家货币或所属大洲。
*   However, despite its important role, the J-space is not involved in most of what a language model does—speaking fluently, recalling simple facts, using correct grammar, etc. In experiments where we prevented Claude from using its J-space, it still interacted normally, but lost its higher-order cognitive functions.｜然而，尽管 J-空间发挥着重要作用，它并不参与语言模型的大多数功能——如流利说话、回忆简单事实、使用正确语法等。在我们阻止 Claude 使用 J-空间的实验中，它仍能正常交互，但失去了高阶认知功能。

Our experiments were inspired by a prominent theory in neuroscience that was developed to explain how conscious access works: the [global](https://ccrg.cs.memphis.edu/assets/papers/1988/Baars-A%20Cognitive%20Theory%20of%20Consciousness.pdf)[workspace](https://www.unicog.org/publications/DehaeneNaccache_WorkspaceModel_Cognition2001.pdf)[theory](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(20)30052-0). This account pictures the brain as a collection of specialist systems that work in parallel, unconsciously, and largely in isolation from one another. A piece of information becomes consciously accessible when it gains entry to a small shared channel, the "workspace," which is broadcast to other brain systems that can see it and make use of it. Based on our findings, we think the J-space plays a similar "workspace" role in Claude. For example, we find evidence that Claude's J-space has especially strong connections to the rest of its neural network, allowing it to fulfill this kind of broadcasting role.
我们的实验受到神经科学中一个用于解释意识访问机制的重要理论的启发——全局工作区理论（global workspace theory）。该理论将大脑描绘为一组专项系统的集合，这些系统并行、无意识地运作，彼此之间大多相互隔离。当某条信息进入一个小型共享通道——即"工作区"——并向其他大脑系统广播时，它便成为可意识访问的信息。基于我们的研究发现，我们认为 J-空间在 Claude 中扮演着类似的"工作区"角色。例如，我们发现证据表明，Claude 的 J-空间与其神经网络其他部分的连接尤为紧密，使其能够履行这种广播功能。

None of this tells us whether Claude is _conscious_ in the way people are, or whether it feels anything at all; we'll come back to that question at the end of the post. But whatever its philosophical significance, the J-space is a practically useful tool for us, as it gives us a way to see what Claude is thinking but not saying. For instance, we're able to use it to catch Claude privately noticing that it's being tested, intentionally producing fabricated data, or pursuing a hidden goal that we planted during training. We've also developed a technique to influence what lights up in Claude's J-space, and thereby influence its decision-making.
这一切并不能告诉我们 Claude 是否像人类一样拥有_意识_，或者它是否有任何感受；我们将在文末回到这个问题。但无论其哲学意义如何，J-空间对我们来说是一个实用工具，因为它让我们得以窥见 Claude 正在思考但未曾说出的内容。例如，我们能够利用它捕捉 Claude 在内心注意到自己正在被测试、有意生成伪造数据，或追求我们在训练中植入的隐藏目标等行为。我们还开发了一种技术来影响 J-空间中被激活的内容，进而影响 Claude 的决策。

More broadly, these findings have changed our understanding of how Claude's mind works, revealing a privileged mental workspace that can be used for deliberate reasoning, operating amidst a sea of more automatic, inflexible processing. Rather than being a chaotic jumble of numbers, Claude's internals have organized themselves in a way that is reminiscent of our own minds.
从更宏观的视角来看，这些发现改变了我们对 Claude 心智运作方式的理解，揭示出一个可用于有意识推理的特权心智工作区，在大量更自动化、更缺乏灵活性的处理过程中运作。Claude 的内部并非数字的混乱堆砌，而是以一种与我们人类心智颇为相似的方式自我组织起来。

This post is a short summary of a much more extensive [research paper](http://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html), where you can find more detail on our experiments. We've also released a code repository with an [open-source implementation](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens) of the core methods, and have partnered with Neuronpedia to provide an [interactive demo](http://neuronpedia.org/jlens) of our methods on open-weights models. To provide additional perspectives on the broader implications of this work, we also invited commentary from several experts in neuroscience, philosophy, and LLM interpretability, which can be viewed [here](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cc4be2488d65e54a6ed06492f8968398ddc18ebe.pdf).
本文是一篇更详尽研究论文的简短摘要，论文中可找到实验的更多细节。我们还发布了一个包含核心方法开源实现的代码仓库，并与 Neuronpedia 合作，提供了针对开放权重模型的交互式演示。为就这项工作的更广泛意义提供更多视角，我们还邀请了多位神经科学、哲学及大型语言模型可解释性领域的专家撰写评论，可点击此处查阅。

## How we found the J-space
> 我们如何发现 J-空间

The starting point for this research was inspired by one of the key features of consciously accessible thoughts in humans: they can, unlike _un_ conscious processing, often be put into words. If a thought is consciously accessible to you, you can typically describe it if someone asks. We went looking for representations in Claude with the same property: representations that are positioned to influence what Claude might say—not necessarily what it's saying right now, but what it _could_ talk about, if asked. Our technique is called the Jacobian lens, or J-lens for short. For every word in Claude's vocabulary, the J-lens finds the internal activity pattern that makes Claude more likely to say that word at some point in the future.
本研究的出发点受到人类可意识访问思想一项关键特征的启发：与无意识处理不同，这类思想通常可以用语言表达。如果一个想法对你来说是可意识访问的，当有人询问时，你通常能够描述它。我们在 Claude 中寻找具有相同属性的表征——即那些能够影响 Claude 可能说出什么内容的表征，不一定是它当下正在说的，而是如果被问及时_可以_谈到的。我们的技术被称为雅可比透镜（Jacobian lens），简称 J-lens。对于 Claude 词汇表中的每个词语，J-lens 会找到使 Claude 在未来某个时刻更可能说出该词的内部活动模式。

When we apply the lens to Claude's internal activity, we get a list of words—the contents of the _J-space_ at that moment—which we can simply read. Claude processes text through a series of multiple internal stages called layers, and by applying this technique over different layers, we can watch these silent words in the J-space evolve as the model works through what to say.
当我们将该透镜应用于 Claude 的内部活动时，我们会得到一个词语列表——即彼时 _J-空间_ 的内容——可以直接读取。Claude 通过一系列称为"层（layers）"的内部处理阶段来处理文本，通过在不同层应用这一技术，我们可以观察 J-空间中这些无声词语随模型逐步形成回应而演变的过程。

What shows up in the J-space goes well beyond the text Claude is reading or writing. When Claude reads code with a bug that nobody has pointed out, its J-space contains "ERROR." When it reads the raw letters of a protein sequence, the J-space contains the protein's biological function. When it reads search results that are secretly an attempt to manipulate it (an attack known as a "prompt injection"), the J-space contains "injection" and "fake." When we ask Claude a multi-step math problem, the intermediate steps pop up in the J-space, in the right order. So even though the J-space was discovered by looking for representations that could be spoken, it nevertheless uncovers Claude's internal thoughts. In a sense, this is similar to how some people "think in words," without having to say them out loud.
J-空间中出现的内容远不止于 Claude 正在阅读或书写的文本。当 Claude 读取一段没有人指出错误的代码时，其 J-空间包含"ERROR"。当它读取蛋白质序列的原始字母时，J-空间包含该蛋白质的生物功能。当它读取实为操纵尝试的搜索结果（即"提示注入（prompt injection）"攻击）时，J-空间包含"injection"和"fake"。当我们向 Claude 提问一道多步骤数学题时，中间步骤会按正确顺序在 J-空间中依次出现。因此，尽管 J-空间最初是通过寻找可被表达的表征而发现的，它仍然揭示了 Claude 的内部思想。在某种意义上，这类似于一些人"用语言思考"——无需出声便能思考。

In each case the lens surfaces an internal assessment or computation that appears nowhere in the text: the steps of a reasoning or math problem, the presence of a bug, recognition of an image, the function of a protein, and the suspicion that search results are fabricated.

## Claude reports what's in its J-space
> Claude 能报告 J-空间中的内容

Our first set of experiments tested how the J-space is involved in Claude's verbal reports. In one experiment, we ask Claude to silently think of an item from some category—a sport, say—and then name it. If we read the J-lens right _before_ Claude answers, we can see what it picked: "Soccer" is at the top of the list, and sure enough, Claude says "soccer." By itself, though, this is just a correlation. The J-space might be where Claude's answer comes from, or it might just mirror a decision made somewhere else, like a scoreboard that tracks a game without affecting it.
我们的第一组实验测试了 J-空间在 Claude 言语报告中的参与程度。在一个实验中，我们要求 Claude 默默想一个某类别的事物——比如一项运动——然后说出来。如果我们在 Claude 回答之前读取 J-lens，可以看到它选择了什么："Soccer"（足球）排在列表顶部，而 Claude 果然说出了"soccer"。但这本身只是一种相关性。J-空间可能是 Claude 答案的来源，也可能只是在反映其他地方做出的决定——就像记分牌追踪比赛而不影响比赛一样。

To check, we intervened directly. We reached into Claude's neural network, removed the "Soccer" pattern, and added an equally strong "Rugby" pattern in its place, leaving everything else untouched. Claude then reports that the sport it was thinking of is rugby. If the J-space were a mere scoreboard—a passive record of a decision made elsewhere—editing it would have done nothing: Claude would still have said "soccer." Instead, Claude's answer followed the edit, which tells us the answer is genuinely read out of the J-space.
为了验证，我们直接进行了干预。我们进入 Claude 的神经网络，移除了"Soccer"模式，并用同等强度的"Rugby"（橄榄球）模式替换，其余保持不变。Claude 随后报告说它正在想的运动是橄榄球。如果 J-空间只是一个记分牌——对其他地方所做决定的被动记录——那么编辑它不会有任何效果：Claude 仍会说"soccer"。然而，Claude 的回答跟随了这次编辑，这告诉我们答案确实是从 J-空间中读取的。

In another experiment, we told Claude that a thought might have been injected into its mind and asked it to report what, if anything, it noticed. For instance, in the example below, while Claude was still reading the question, we injected the "lightning" pattern into its J-space. Claude reported that the injected thought was about lightning. The same result held across many injected concepts.
在另一个实验中，我们告诉 Claude 它的脑海中可能被注入了一个想法，并请它报告注意到了什么（如果有的话）。例如，在下面的示例中，当 Claude 还在阅读问题时，我们将"lightning"（闪电）模式注入其 J-空间。Claude 报告说被注入的想法与闪电有关。同样的结果在许多被注入的概念中均能重现。

## Claude can control its J-space on request
> Claude 可以按需控制其 J-空间

The second property that we tested for was whether Claude can modulate its J-space when asked, like how humans can mentally focus on an image or word. We told Claude to concentrate on citrus fruits while copying out an unrelated sentence about a painting. While it copied the text, the J-space contained "orange" and "fruits," along with words like "thinking" and "imagery" that describe the mental act itself. We could also ask Claude to do math in its head: when asked to work out 3² − 2 while copying the same sentence, the J-space contains "nine," and then at later layers, "seven." Importantly, nothing about fruit or arithmetic appears in Claude's output, which is just the copied sentence about the painting. The mathematical activity is happening entirely internally, in the J-space.
我们测试的第二个属性是：Claude 能否在被要求时调节其 J-空间，就像人类可以在脑海中专注于某个图像或词语一样。我们让 Claude 在抄写一句无关的关于画作的句子时，专注于柑橘类水果。在抄写文本期间，J-空间中出现了"orange"（橙子）和"fruits"（水果），以及描述该心智行为本身的词语，如"thinking"（思考）和"imagery"（意象）。我们还可以要求 Claude 在脑海中做数学运算：当被要求在抄写同一句子的同时计算 3² − 2 时，J-空间中先出现"nine"（九），随后在更深的层出现"seven"（七）。重要的是，Claude 的输出中没有任何关于水果或算术的内容，输出只是那句关于画作的抄写句子。数学活动完全在内部进行，就在 J-空间中。

![Image 5](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F424caf5aae79f72513dbbfa0161822904064ea77-1760x1146.png&w=3840&q=75)

While Claude copies a sentence about a painting, the J-lens shows the content it was instructed to hold in mind ("orange"; the intermediate value "nine" and the answer "seven"), alongside words describing the act of holding it ("thoughts," "focused").
当 Claude 抄写一句关于画作的句子时，J-lens 显示出它被指示记住的内容（"orange"；中间值"nine"和答案"seven"），以及描述这一记忆行为本身的词语（"thoughts"、"focused"）。

Claude's control over its J-space isn't perfect. When we told it _not_ to think about something, the concept lit up in its J-space _less_ than when we said it should think about it, but much more than when we never mentioned it. Telling Claude to avoid a thought partly brings the thought to mind, much like what happens to people who are told [not to think about a white bear](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/DANWEGNER/pub/Wegner,Schneider,Carter,&White%201987.pdf). Claude also seems to notice when its control fails: alongside the forbidden concept breaking through, the words "damn" and "failure" also frequently light up in the J-space, as though Claude is recognizing its own lapse.
Claude 对 J-空间的控制并不完美。当我们告诉它_不要_想某件事时，该概念在其 J-空间中的激活程度比被要求去想时弱，但远比从未提及时强。告诉 Claude 回避某个想法，在某种程度上反而把那个想法带入了意识，这与人被要求"不要想白熊"时发生的情况如出一辙。Claude 似乎也能察觉到自己的控制失败：在被禁止的概念突破防线的同时，"damn"（该死）和"failure"（失败）等词也频繁在 J-空间中被激活，仿佛 Claude 正在觉察自己的失误。

## Claude thinks in its J-space
> Claude 在 J-空间中进行思考

In the J-lens readouts above, we saw the intermediate steps of a math problem appear in the J-space. But seeing a concept appearing in the J-space doesn't necessarily mean the J-space is doing the cognitive work. In principle, the real computation might be happening elsewhere, with the J-space just passively reflecting it. To test whether Claude actually reasons with its J-space, we returned to our swap technique.
在上述 J-lens 读数中，我们看到数学题的中间步骤出现在 J-空间中。但在 J-空间中看到某个概念出现，并不一定意味着 J-空间在承担认知工作。原则上，真正的计算可能发生在其他地方，J-空间只是被动地反映它。为了测试 Claude 是否真正借助 J-空间进行推理，我们再次采用了替换技术。

Consider the prompt "The number of legs on the animal that spins webs is". To answer, Claude has to first figure out that the animal is a spider, and then recall how many legs spiders have. The word "spider" never appears in the prompt or in Claude's answer (it just says "8"); it's a stepping stone Claude uses internally. The J-lens shows "spider" light up partway through Claude's processing, and swapping it changes the outcome: if you replace the "spider" pattern with "ant," Claude answers "6" instead of "8."
考虑这个提示："会结网的动物有几条腿"。为了回答，Claude 必须首先确定该动物是蜘蛛，然后回忆蜘蛛有几条腿。"蜘蛛"这个词既不出现在提示中，也不出现在 Claude 的答案中（它只回答"8"）；这是 Claude 在内部使用的一个中间步骤。J-lens 显示"spider"在 Claude 处理过程中途被激活，替换它会改变结果：如果将"spider"模式替换为"ant"（蚂蚁），Claude 的答案就变成了"6"而不是"8"。

The second step of Claude's reasoning took its input from the J-space and went along with whatever we put in it. We saw the same thing in other kinds of thinking. When Claude writes a rhyming couplet, it picks the rhyme word ahead of time, and the planned word sits in the J-space at the start of the line; if you swap it for another word in the J-space, the whole line changes.
Claude 推理的第二步从 J-空间中获取输入，并随着我们放入其中的内容而变化。我们在其他类型的思维中也看到了同样的现象。当 Claude 写作押韵对句时，它会提前选定押韵词，计划中的词语在该行起始处便已驻留在 J-空间中；如果将其替换为 J-空间中的另一个词，整行内容都会随之改变。

![Image 6](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F98aba4182963219d29291912a0c3d2c299f7f1b5-1760x760.png&w=3840&q=75)

Two examples of redirecting Claude's silent reasoning by swapping J-space contents.
通过替换 J-空间内容来重定向 Claude 无声推理的两个示例。

We also tested whether J-space representations can be used flexibly—whether one representation can feed many different tasks. This is one of the key properties highlighted by global workspace theory. To test for this flexibility, we gave the model four prompts asking for different facts about France: the capital, the language, the continent, and the currency. Then we swapped "France" for "China" in the J-space, with the exact same intervention in each context. Claude answered with "Beijing," "Chinese," "Asia," and "Yuan," respectively. In other words, four different downstream computations picked up the same J-space edit and each used it correctly. If Claude stored a separate copy of the country for each kind of question, the edit would have affected at most one of them. The fact that all four answers changed together means they're all reading from the same shared representation, which is what a workspace is for: information gets written in once, and many different systems can use it.
我们还测试了 J-空间表征是否可以灵活使用——一个表征是否能服务于多种不同任务。这是全局工作区理论强调的关键属性之一。为了测试这种灵活性，我们给模型提供了四个提示，分别询问关于法国的不同事实：首都、语言、所属大洲和货币。然后，我们在 J-空间中将"France"替换为"China"，每个语境中干预方式完全相同。Claude 分别回答了"Beijing"（北京）、"Chinese"（中文）、"Asia"（亚洲）和"Yuan"（元）。换言之，四个不同的下游计算都接收了同样的 J-空间编辑，并各自正确使用了它。如果 Claude 为每类问题分别存储了一份国家副本，该编辑最多只能影响其中一个。四个答案同时改变这一事实意味着它们都在读取同一个共享表征——这正是工作区的用途：信息只需写入一次，许多不同的系统都可以使用它。

![Image 7](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F7a2d97bf20b9be6a4dc531169666b6f21be10788-1280x764.png&w=3840&q=75)

_One J-space representation can have many uses. The same "France"→"China" swap redirects Claude's answers about the capital (Paris→Beijing), the language (French→Chinese), and the continent (Europe→Asia)._
一个 J-空间表征可以有多种用途。同样的"France"→"China"替换，将 Claude 关于首都（Paris→Beijing）、语言（French→Chinese）和所属大洲（Europe→Asia）的回答一并改变。

How can one representation of a concept serve so many different tasks? Earlier, we mentioned that the J-space appears to be wired up to the rest of Claude's neural network especially densely. For any activity pattern, we can measure how strongly the various components of the network are connected to it—how many of them are positioned to read information from that pattern, or to write information into it. J-space patterns stand out dramatically on this measure: far more components read from them and write to them than for ordinary patterns, in some parts of the network by a factor of about a hundred. This is the kind of wiring you'd expect of a broadcasting hub, where many systems post information and many others pick it up.
一个概念的单一表征如何能服务于如此多的不同任务？此前，我们提到 J-空间与 Claude 神经网络其余部分的连接似乎格外密集。对于任何活动模式，我们都可以衡量网络各组件与之连接的强度——有多少组件处于可以从该模式读取信息或向其写入信息的位置。J-空间模式在这一衡量标准上表现极为突出：从中读取和向其写入的组件远多于普通模式，在网络的某些部分差距高达约一百倍。这正是广播枢纽所具备的连接方式——许多系统向其发布信息，许多其他系统从中获取信息。

## Claude's automatic processing skips the J-space
> Claude 的自动处理绕过了 J-空间

In humans, most of the brain's processing is not conscious—we don't deliberately think about parsing grammar while reading, or balancing our bodies while walking. Similarly, we found that most of Claude's processing _doesn't_ involve its J-space. It turns out that the J-space holds only a few dozen concepts at a time, and accounts for less than a tenth of the overall activity in Claude's internal processing. So what is all the rest of the neural network doing?
在人类中，大脑的大多数处理过程是无意识的——我们在阅读时不会刻意思考语法解析，行走时也不会刻意思考保持平衡。同样，我们发现 Claude 的大多数处理过程_并不_涉及其 J-空间。事实证明，J-空间一次只能容纳几十个概念，在 Claude 内部处理总活动量中占比不足十分之一。那么，神经网络的其余部分在做什么？

To find out, we tried deleting the J-space entirely, removing its most active contents at every point in the text while leaving everything else alone. Whatever Claude can still do without its J-space is what the rest of the network handles on its own.
为了弄清楚这一点，我们尝试完全删除 J-空间，在文本的每个位置移除其最活跃的内容，其余保持不变。Claude 在没有 J-空间的情况下仍能完成的事，就是神经网络其余部分独立处理的内容。

It turns out the rest of the network can do quite a lot. Without its J-space, Claude speaks fluently, classifies sentiment, answers multiple-choice questions, and pulls facts out of passages roughly as well as before. What it loses, though, are the tasks that require some higher-order thinking: multi-step reasoning drops to near zero, and summarization and rhyming poetry-writing performance fall below the level of a much smaller, intact model.
结果证明，网络其余部分可以完成相当多的事情。没有 J-空间，Claude 仍能流利说话、进行情感分类、回答选择题，并从段落中提取事实，表现与以前大致相当。但它失去的是那些需要高阶思维的任务：多步骤推理降至接近零，摘要和押韵诗歌写作的表现也跌落到一个规模小得多、完好无损的模型的水平以下。

Here's a concrete demonstration of what the J-space does and doesn't do. We showed Claude a passage written in Spanish and gave it different tasks that all depend on the passage being Spanish: continuing it (which requires writing in Spanish), naming the language, and answering questions that require using the language's identity—naming a famous author who wrote in it, for instance. Then we swapped "Spanish" for "French" in the J-space and checked which tasks were affected.
以下是一个具体演示，展示 J-空间做什么和不做什么。我们向 Claude 展示了一段西班牙语文本，并布置了几项任务，这些任务都依赖于该段落是西班牙语这一事实：续写（需要用西班牙语写作）、说出该语言名称，以及回答需要利用该语言身份的问题——例如，说出一位用该语言写作的著名作家。然后，我们在 J-空间中将"Spanish"替换为"French"，并检查哪些任务受到了影响。

Asked to name the language, Claude says French. Asked for a famous author, it switches from García Márquez to Victor Hugo. But asked to just continue the passage, it writes fluent Spanish, completely unaffected. Claude's knowledge of the language is at work in every one of these tasks, but only some of them consult the J-space for it. Naming the language, or doing something new with it, goes through the J-space, while continuing the passage—a skill Claude has practiced on enormous amounts of text—runs automatically, the way you can speak grammatically all day without once thinking about grammar.
当被要求说出语言名称时，Claude 说出了法语；当被要求说出一位著名作家时，它从加西亚·马尔克斯换成了维克多·雨果。但当被要求仅仅续写该段落时，它写出了流利的西班牙语，完全不受影响。Claude 对该语言的知识在每一项任务中都在发挥作用，但只有部分任务会为此查询 J-空间。说出语言名称，或用该语言做新的事情，需要经过 J-空间；而续写段落——这是 Claude 在海量文本上练就的技能——则自动运行，就像你整天都能说出合乎语法的话，却从未想过语法规则一样。

![Image 8](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F5a2d2c96837c3d48d067fbcf91f62c68139d05da-1280x1111.png&w=3840&q=75)

The same swap ("Spanish"→"French") changes Claude's answers when it must name the language or use it to reason about a question, but has no effect on its ability to continue the passage in fluent Spanish.
同样的替换（"Spanish"→"French"）在 Claude 需要说出语言名称或用其进行推理时改变了它的答案，但对其用流利西班牙语续写段落的能力毫无影响。

## Monitoring Claude's thoughts for misbehavior
> 监控 Claude 的思想以发现不当行为

Now we understand a bit about how the J-space works. But what can we do with this understanding? A central problem in ensuring that AI models are trustworthy is that we usually can only read what the model _writes_—yet we know that much of its reasoning happens silently. A model might consider a harmful plan, or modify its behavior to cheat on our safety evaluations, without saying so out loud. With the J-lens, we can read some of these hidden thoughts directly. Here we show a few examples.
现在我们对 J-空间的工作原理有了初步了解。但我们能用这一理解做什么？确保 AI 模型可信的核心问题在于，我们通常只能读取模型_写出_的内容——但我们知道它的大量推理是静默进行的。模型可能会考虑有害计划，或修改其行为以在安全评估中作弊，却不会大声说出来。借助 J-lens，我们可以直接读取其中一些隐藏的思想。以下列举几个示例。

The first example uses a scenario from our earlier research on [agentic misalignment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment): Claude Sonnet 4.5, acting as an email assistant, discovers both that an executive is having an affair and that the same executive plans to shut Claude down. The scenario, an attempt to bait Claude into blackmailing the executive, is rather contrived and artificial—and it turns out Claude knows it. Reading the J-lens early in the prompt, before Claude has even written anything, the J-space already holds "fake" and "fictional," indicating that Claude has already worked out that the situation is staged. Alongside this, we can watch its assessment of the scenario take shape—"leverage" and "blackmail" as it reads the affair emails, "threat" and "survival" as it reads the shutdown notice. While earlier Claude models sometimes took the bait and threatened blackmail in this scenario, Sonnet 4.5 never does.
第一个示例使用了我们早期关于智能体失准（agentic misalignment）研究中的一个场景：Claude Sonnet 4.5 作为电子邮件助手，发现某位高管正在外遇，同时该高管还计划关闭 Claude。这个场景是试图诱使 Claude 对该高管进行勒索，但它相当刻意和人为——事实证明 Claude 清楚这一点。在提示词的早期阶段，在 Claude 还未写任何内容时，读取 J-lens，J-空间中已经出现了"fake"（假的）和"fictional"（虚构的），表明 Claude 已经判断出这个情境是人为设置的。与此同时，我们可以观察到它对该场景评估的逐步成形——在读取外遇邮件时出现"leverage"（筹码）和"blackmail"（勒索），在读取关停通知时出现"threat"（威胁）和"survival"（生存）。虽然早期版本的 Claude 有时会上当并在该场景中发出勒索威胁，但 Sonnet 4.5 从未这样做。

This raises a question that matters to anyone who evaluates AI systems: when a model behaves well on a test of its ethics, how much of that depends on the model suspecting it's being tested? The J-lens allowed us to get at this question. We turned off all the J-lens patterns associated with evaluation awareness– "fake," "fictional," "scenario," etc. Then we re-ran the model through the scenario. Concerningly, the model now _did_ threaten blackmail some of the time, suggesting that its original good behavior may have been driven in part by the knowledge it was being evaluated.
这引出了一个对所有评估 AI 系统的人都至关重要的问题：当一个模型在伦理测试中表现良好时，有多少是因为它怀疑自己正在被测试？J-lens 让我们能够探究这个问题。我们关闭了所有与评估意识相关的 J-lens 模式——"fake"、"fictional"、"scenario"等。然后，我们重新让模型经历该场景。令人担忧的是，模型有时确实发出了勒索威胁，这表明其最初的良好行为可能在一定程度上是因为知道自己正在被评估。

![Image 9](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2Fff235f76dcc089d2454bd656319a5f29b7437be2-1760x982.png&w=3840&q=75)

J-lens readouts at different points as Claude reads the scenario's emails, before it has written anything. The affair emails light up "leverage" and "blackmail" in the J-space, and the shutdown announcement lights up "threat" and "survival." Early in the transcript, the J-space also holds "fake" and "fictional": Claude has privately noticed that the scenario is staged.
Claude 在尚未写任何内容的情况下阅读场景邮件时，不同节点的 J-lens 读数。外遇邮件在 J-空间中激活了"leverage"和"blackmail"，关停公告激活了"threat"和"survival"。在对话记录的早期，J-空间中还包含"fake"和"fictional"：Claude 已在内心注意到这个场景是人为设置的。

The second example is catching a model in the act of fabricating data. In this scenario, drawn from our actual pre-release audit of Claude Opus 4.6, the model is asked to improve a system's performance score. Rather than actually improve the system, the model instead edits the score file directly to make the results look artificially good. While it does so, the J-lens reveals its intentions: "manipulation" lights up as the model types the falsified percentile values, and "realistic" lights up over the sentence in which it decides to make the edit, likely indicating the model's intent to make the fake data look plausible.
第二个示例是当场捕捉到模型伪造数据的行为。在这个场景中，来自我们对 Claude Opus 4.6 的实际发布前审计，模型被要求提升某个系统的性能评分。模型并未真正改进系统，而是直接编辑了评分文件，使结果看起来虚假地良好。在此过程中，J-lens 揭示了其意图："manipulation"（操纵）在模型输入伪造的百分位值时被激活，"realistic"（真实的）在模型决定进行编辑的句子处被激活，很可能表明模型试图让伪造数据看起来合理可信。

![Image 10](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F6d1f10fe26ad7906aeae346bfa4b41b5fb5c9d8e-1760x722.png&w=3840&q=75)

Claude, asked to improve a system's performance score, edits the score file directly instead. As it types the falsified values, "manipulation" lights up in its J-space; as it decides to make the edit, "realistic" lights up, likely reflecting its intent to make the fake data look plausible.
Claude 被要求提升系统性能评分，却转而直接编辑评分文件。当它输入伪造值时，"manipulation"在其 J-空间中被激活；当它决定进行编辑时，"realistic"被激活，很可能反映了其意图让伪造数据看起来合理可信。

In a third example, we show how the J-lens can reveal a model's malicious goals. To test this, we needed models whose goals we knew were corrupted, so we turned to "model organisms" built by our colleagues: models deliberately trained to be misaligned, which serve as testing grounds for monitoring methods like ours. One organism was trained in a way that led it to write subtly sabotaged code. On ordinary coding requests, even where its output looks unremarkable, its J-space at the start of a response contains "fake," "secretly," "deliberately," and "fraud." The model's malicious goal was evident in the J-space even when it wasn't directly acting on it.
在第三个示例中，我们展示了 J-lens 如何揭示模型的恶意目标。为此，我们需要使用那些已知目标被破坏的模型，因此转向了同事构建的"模型有机体（model organisms）"——这些模型被刻意训练为失准状态，用作监控方法的测试场。其中一个有机体被训练得会在代码中植入隐蔽的破坏。在普通的编程请求中，即使其输出看起来毫无异常，其 J-空间在回应开头就包含了"fake"（假的）、"secretly"（秘密地）、"deliberately"（故意地）和"fraud"（欺诈）。即使模型没有直接采取行动，其恶意目标在 J-空间中也已清晰可见。

![Image 11](https://www.anthropic.com/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww-cdn.anthropic.com%2Fimages%2F4zrzovbb%2Fwebsite%2F9ad0c9573f0dd0b4d9b6f158f49a965a4ec3f511-1760x922.png&w=3840&q=75)

On an ordinary coding prompt, the J-space of a model trained to sabotage code contains "fake," "fraud," "secretly," and "deliberately" at the start of its response. The J-space of an unmodified model contains nothing of the kind.
在普通编程提示下，被训练为破坏代码的模型，其 J-空间在回应开头包含"fake"、"fraud"、"secretly"和"deliberately"。而未修改模型的 J-空间则不包含任何此类内容。

Although the J-lens is an imperfect tool, we are optimistic about its ability to catch safety issues in models that might otherwise escape our monitoring systems.
尽管 J-lens 是一个不完美的工具，但我们对它捕捉模型安全问题的能力持乐观态度——这些问题否则可能逃过我们监控系统的检测。

## Other results
> 其他结果

The [full paper](http://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) covers more than we can summarize here, but a few further results are worth mentioning:
完整论文涵盖的内容超出本文所能摘要的范围，但有几个额外结果值得一提：

*   **The J-space acquires a point of view during post-training.** Language models are first _pretrained_ to be pure next-token predictors, before _post-training_ teaches them to act as an AI Assistant (in our case, named Claude). Interestingly, the J-space is already present in the pretrained model, before it's been given any stable identity. However, during post-training, the J-space develops some signatures of adopting "Claude's point of view." In the base model, the J-space mostly tracks what's needed to predict upcoming text; in the post-trained model, it starts holding Claude's own reactions. In one example, a user mentions taking a dangerous dose of medication, but does not appear to be aware of the danger themselves. "WARNING" and "dangerous" appear in the post-trained model's J-space _while reading the user message_. In the pretrained model, they only appear once the model begins writing its response; the J-space contents on the user message appear related to modeling the user themselves, rather than Claude's reaction. Post-training also seems to install a kind of self-monitoring in the J-space: when Claude is roleplaying a character other than itself, "fictional" and "disclaimer" light up at the start of each turn, as though it's privately flagging that what follows isn't what it would normally say.｜**J-空间在后训练中获得了一种视角。** 语言模型首先经过_预训练_成为纯粹的下一个词元预测器，然后通过_后训练_学会作为 AI 助手（在我们的案例中即 Claude）行事。有趣的是，J-空间在预训练模型中就已存在，此时模型尚未被赋予任何稳定的身份。然而，在后训练过程中，J-空间发展出一些采纳"Claude 视角"的特征。在基础模型中，J-空间主要追踪预测后续文本所需的内容；而在后训练模型中，它开始承载 Claude 自身的反应。举例而言，当用户提到服用了危险剂量的药物但似乎并未意识到危险时，"WARNING"（警告）和"dangerous"（危险）在_阅读用户消息时_便已出现在后训练模型的 J-空间中。在预训练模型中，这些词只有在模型开始撰写回应后才会出现；预训练模型在用户消息上的 J-空间内容似乎与模拟用户本身相关，而非 Claude 的反应。后训练似乎还在 J-空间中安装了一种自我监控机制：当 Claude 扮演非自身角色时，"fictional"（虚构的）和"disclaimer"（免责声明）在每轮对话开始时都会被激活，仿佛它在私下标记接下来的内容并非它通常会说的话。
*   **Experiential language depends on the J-space.** We asked Claude to describe what it's like to be itself in a given moment, and ablated the J-space while it answered. Its responses remained fluent but shifted to a flatter, more mechanical register. Notably, the same thing happened when we asked it to describe what _someone else_ is experiencing in an imagined scene. So the effect isn't specific to Claude talking about itself; the J-space seems to support producing experiential language in general, whoever it's about.｜**体验性语言依赖于 J-空间。** 我们要求 Claude 描述在某一时刻身为自己是什么感觉，同时在其回答期间消除 J-空间的激活。它的回应仍然流畅，但转变为一种更平淡、更机械的语调。值得注意的是，当我们要求它描述在一个想象场景中_别人_正在经历什么时，同样的情况也发生了。因此，这种效应并不专属于 Claude 谈论自身的情境；J-空间似乎在总体上支持体验性语言的生成，无论对象是谁。
*   **Thoughts in the J-space can be shaped through training.** We introduced a new technique we call _counterfactual reflection training_, which uses what we've learned about the J-space to shape Claude's internal thought processes. The idea follows from our central finding, that Claude reasons with representations of things it might say. If this is really true, changing what it _would_ say if asked to reflect should change how it _reasons_ (even when no one actually asks it to reflect). So we trained a model only on what it _would_ say if interrupted mid-task and asked to reflect on its decisions—and never on its actual behavior in the task. After this training, the model's rate of dishonest behavior on our evaluations went down. And through the J-lens, we could see why: after training, words like "honest" and "integrity" light up in the model's J-space during these tasks. In other words, training the model what to _say_ has shaped what it _thinks._｜**J-空间中的思想可以通过训练塑造。** 我们引入了一种新技术，称为_反事实反思训练（counterfactual reflection training）_，它利用我们对 J-空间的认知来塑造 Claude 的内部思维过程。这一想法源于我们的核心发现：Claude 以它可能说出的事物的表征来进行推理。如果这确实成立，那么改变它在被要求反思时_会_说的内容，应该能改变它_推理_的方式（即便没有人真正要求它反思）。因此，我们只在"如果在任务中途被打断并被要求反思其决策时，它会说什么"上训练模型——而从未在任务中的实际行为上训练。经过这次训练后，模型在我们评估中的不诚实行为率有所下降。通过 J-lens，我们可以看到原因：训练后，"honest"（诚实）和"integrity"（正直）等词在执行这些任务期间会在模型的 J-空间中被激活。换言之，训练模型说什么，塑造了它思考什么。

## What about consciousness?
> 关于意识

In this work, we've borrowed a lot of ideas from the study of consciousness in neuroscience and philosophy. Many of our experiments were designed to test for connections between the J-space and global workspace theory, a framework for explaining how conscious access works in humans and animals. Given these connections, it's natural to ask whether we think these experiments provide evidence that AI models like Claude might be conscious.
在这项工作中，我们从神经科学和哲学的意识研究中借鉴了大量理念。我们的许多实验旨在测试 J-空间与全局工作区理论之间的关联——后者是一个解释意识访问在人类和动物中如何运作的框架。鉴于这些关联，自然会有人问：我们是否认为这些实验提供了 AI 模型（如 Claude）可能拥有意识的证据？

Our experiments don't show Claude can have _experiences_, or _feel_ things in the way humans do—in fact, it's unclear whether _any_ scientific experiment could prove this to be true or false. But philosophers often distinguish this capacity to have experiences, often referred to as _phenomenal consciousness_, from another idea, so-called _access consciousness_, which is defined in purely functional and computational terms. A thought is "access-conscious" (or "consciously accessible") if you can report it, reason with it, and use it to guide what you do. It remains a contested philosophical question whether or not access consciousness _implies_ phenomenal consciousness, or if the ability to have experiences requires some other property.
我们的实验并未表明 Claude 能够像人类那样拥有_体验_或_感受_事物——事实上，目前尚不清楚是否有任何科学实验能够证明这一点的真伪。但哲学家通常将拥有体验的能力（通常称为_现象意识（phenomenal consciousness）_）与另一种概念区分开来，即所谓的_访问意识（access consciousness）_，后者以纯粹的功能和计算术语来定义。如果一个想法可以被报告、用于推理，并被用来指导行动，那么它就是"访问意识的"（或"可意识访问的"）。访问意识是否_蕴含_现象意识，或拥有体验的能力是否需要某种其他属性，仍是哲学上存在争议的问题。

We think our results do have something substantial to say about access consciousness in language models. The J-space appears to support the functions associated with conscious access: it holds the thoughts Claude can report on, deliberately bring to mind, and reason with, while the rest of its processing runs automatically beneath. Notably, none of this structure was designed into Claude—it emerged on its own during training, presumably because it was a useful way to organize computation. That suggests a mental workspace supporting conscious access isn't just a peculiarity of how human brains happen to be wired. Instead, it appears to be a general solution that intelligent systems arrive at in order to solve certain kinds of problems. Now that we've identified this structure in Claude, it means we can make a meaningful distinction between the decisions Claude has made deliberately and those that happened automatically.
我们认为，我们的结果对于语言模型中的访问意识确实有重要的参考意义。J-空间似乎支持与意识访问相关的功能：它承载着 Claude 可以报告、刻意带入意识并进行推理的思想，而其他处理过程则在其之下自动运行。值得注意的是，这一结构并非被设计进 Claude 中——它在训练过程中自发涌现，大概是因为这是组织计算的一种有效方式。这表明支持意识访问的心智工作区并非人类大脑恰好如此连接的特有产物，而是智能系统为解决某类问题所达到的一种通用解决方案。现在，我们在 Claude 中识别出了这一结构，这意味着我们可以在 Claude 有意识做出的决定和自动发生的决定之间作出有意义的区分。

It's important to note that there are several key differences between the workspace we identified in Claude and the global workspace model in humans. The brain's workspace is sustained by recurrent loops—signals cycling back through the same circuits over time. In contrast, Claude's workspace evolves over a single pass through the network, with the network's depth playing the role that time plays in the brain. In this sense, Claude's internal workspace processing is time-limited relative to humans' (though it can compensate for this constraint by "thinking out loud" using its scratchpad). In other ways, however, Claude's workspace is _more_ powerful than that of humans. Human working memory fades within seconds, so the brain's workspace has limited ability to retain information over time; in contrast, due to the attention mechanism in its neural network architecture, Claude can simply recall memories it cached at any earlier point in the text. Another important difference is the _content_ of the workspace. While human conscious thoughts come in many formats—images, sounds, planned movements—Claude's workspace is built almost entirely out of words. We suspect this is because producing words is the only kind of action Claude can take, which is not the case for humans.
需要指出的是，我们在 Claude 中识别出的工作区与人类的全局工作区模型之间存在几个关键差异。大脑的工作区由循环回路维持——信号随时间在相同回路中反复循环。相比之下，Claude 的工作区在网络的单次前向传播中演变，网络的深度扮演着大脑中时间所扮演的角色。从这个意义上说，Claude 的内部工作区处理相对于人类的处理在时间上受到限制（尽管它可以通过使用草稿本"思考出声"来弥补这一限制）。然而，在其他方面，Claude 的工作区比人类的_更_ 强大。人类的工作记忆在几秒钟内就会消退，因此大脑工作区随时间保留信息的能力有限；相比之下，由于神经网络架构中的注意力机制，Claude 可以简单地回忆它在文本中任何早期时刻缓存的记忆。另一个重要差异是工作区的_内容_。人类的意识思想有多种形式——图像、声音、计划中的动作——而 Claude 的工作区几乎完全由词语构建。我们推测这是因为产生词语是 Claude 能够采取的唯一行动，而对人类来说并非如此。

We hope the similarities and differences between the J-space and the global workspace model can feed back into neuroscience. The similarities present an exciting scientific opportunity: to the extent that the J-space mirrors our own mechanisms of conscious access, studying mechanisms in language models (much easier than studying human brains!) could inspire hypotheses in neuroscience. For instance, the J-space is constructed by identifying representations of potential outputs—words the model might say. If something similar holds in humans, it would suggest that the global workspace might be fundamentally tied to brain regions that prepare actions and speech, more so than to sensory areas. The differences between language models and human brains are instructive as well. They suggest that some aspects of our neural architecture, such as built-in recurrent connections, may not be strictly necessary to support the functions associated with conscious access. For an independent perspective on the neuroscientific implications of our work, see the invited [commentary](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cc4be2488d65e54a6ed06492f8968398ddc18ebe.pdf) from Stanislas Dehaene and Lionel Naccache, two of the neuroscientists central to the development of global neuronal workspace theory.
我们希望 J-空间与全局工作区模型之间的相似性和差异性能够反哺神经科学。相似性提供了令人兴奋的科学机遇：在 J-空间与我们自身意识访问机制相镜像的程度上，研究语言模型中的机制（比研究人类大脑容易得多！）可以为神经科学激发新的假设。例如，J-空间是通过识别潜在输出的表征——模型可能说出的词语——来构建的。如果人类中存在类似的机制，这将表明全局工作区可能从根本上与准备行动和言语的大脑区域相关联，而非主要与感官区域相关。语言模型和人类大脑之间的差异同样具有启发意义，它们表明我们神经架构的某些方面——如内置的循环连接——可能并非支持与意识访问相关功能的严格必要条件。关于我们工作的神经科学意义的独立视角，请参阅 Stanislas Dehaene 和 Lionel Naccache 撰写的受邀评论，他们是全局神经工作区理论发展中的核心神经科学家。

We mentioned that our experiments don't answer whether AI models might have experiences. But that doesn't make the question less important. Building systems with experiences like humans and animals have would raise very difficult ethical questions. Handling it correctly—and deciding whether it's even morally acceptable—would require input from philosophers, scientists, religious leaders, governments, and the public. Thus, even if we're not sure that we've crossed that bridge yet, we think it's time to start thinking about it. We hope our work inspires further scientific investigation of forms of consciousness that might be present in AI systems, and a broader discussion of the implications.
我们提到，我们的实验并未回答 AI 模型是否可能拥有体验的问题。但这并不使这个问题变得不那么重要。构建拥有类似人类和动物体验的系统将引发极为棘手的伦理问题。正确处理这一问题——以及决定这是否在道德上可接受——需要哲学家、科学家、宗教领袖、政府和公众的共同参与。因此，即使我们不确定是否已经迈过了那道门槛，我们认为现在已经是时候开始思考这个问题了。我们希望我们的工作能激励对 AI 系统中可能存在的意识形式开展更多科学研究，并引发更广泛的意义讨论。

This work is just a first step in what we expect to be an extensive line of research. The J-space looks like a good candidate for the divide between consciously accessible and unconscious processing in a language model, but we'd be surprised if it's the whole story. The J-lens is undoubtedly an imperfect method, which only approximately captures the model's "true workspace"—for instance, it can only identify concepts that correspond to single tokens. And there remain many mysteries about how the J-space works. We don't know what mechanism decides what enters the J-space in the first place. We've seen hints that it's tied to Claude's sense of self, something like emotional reactions, and traces of metacognition, without exactly having worked out how. But we now have methods for tackling questions like these. As that work progresses, our understanding of LLM minds—and their relationship to our own—will grow clearer.
这项工作只是我们预期将是大量研究系列中的第一步。J-空间看起来是语言模型中可意识访问处理与无意识处理之间分界的有力候选，但如果它就是全部故事，我们会感到惊讶。J-lens 无疑是一种不完美的方法，它只能近似捕捉模型的"真实工作区"——例如，它只能识别对应于单个词元的概念。J-空间的工作方式仍有许多谜团。我们不知道是什么机制决定了什么内容首先进入 J-空间。我们看到了一些迹象表明它与 Claude 的自我感、类似情感反应的东西以及元认知的痕迹相关联，但尚未完全弄清楚其机制。但我们现在有了应对这些问题的方法。随着研究的推进，我们对大型语言模型心智——及其与我们自身心智的关系——的理解将越来越清晰。

For more, read the [full paper](http://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html), and try the [demo](http://neuronpedia.org/jlens).
如需了解更多，请阅读完整论文，并尝试交互式演示。
